# Phase II-F synthetic maintenance policy

The only executable intervention in this phase is `SYNTHETIC-MAINTENANCE/preventive_replacement` for `MILL_01` or `PMP_01`. It lasts 15 minutes, makes that asset unavailable, and resets its simulated wear to 0.02 at completion. A seeded successor life is drawn from the existing asset wear distribution. Pump service requires the entire IF heat to remain outside the service interval. Mill service excludes rolling. These are explicit assumptions in [maintenance_v1.yaml](../configs/maintenance_v1.yaml), because the reference specification contains no site-validated service duration, cost, or wear-reset effect. There is no maintenance direct-cost estimate and no financial ROI claim.

The frozen Phase II-B calibrated 24-hour risk is a trigger candidate, not an instruction to shut down. Public vibration quality must cover at least three of the latest four closed interval rows, and the asset must not already be in a posted repair/service state. A service may be considered at 00:00, 04:00, 08:00, 12:00, 16:00 or 20:00 if that window is still in the future and physically available. The MILP chooses zero or one service per asset. It first finds robust feasibility under all inherited duration scenarios, then minimizes an explicitly labelled expected downtime-minute heuristic, then minimizes synthetic electricity tariff cost among plans at that minimum. The heuristic assumes conditional failure time is uniform within 24 hours; it is not a calibrated failure-time distribution.

The development pilot used only seeds 2001/day 14 and 2002/day 12. Both physically valid no-service references had a corrective repair (0.429 and 0.482 hours respectively). Three predeclared policies all yielded 2/2 physically valid full replays and 237.12 t combined bars. The 0.55 and 0.70 one-observation policies each executed two services with 0.50 combined hours of preventive plus corrective unavailability. The 0.55 two-observation policy executed one service and had 0.679 hours combined unavailability. The two one-observation policies had identical physical outcomes in this pilot; the first declared candidate, 0.55, was selected by the documented tie order. The selected policy has a small-sample basis and is **not** validated as a universally optimal threshold.

Eligibility does not force service. With the frozen repair-duration range of 15–30 minutes, the mean corrective duration is 22.5 minutes. Under the declared planning heuristic, an immediate 15-minute service only reduces expected unavailability when `p24 × 22.5 > 15`, or approximately `p24 > 0.667`; a later service has a higher threshold because exposure before the service remains. Physical feasibility can still preclude it. This consequence calculation is independent of the 0.55 eligibility gate and is an explicit synthetic assumption, not a learned failure-time law. MILL and PMP thresholds are separate configuration fields; their selected values happen to be equal in this small pilot.

The immutable [design freeze](../replay/maintenance_v1/design_freeze.json) records the chosen thresholds, sensor gate, two pilot seeds, selection rule, split hash, config hash, and source hashes before validation or final evaluation. Later seeds may test this design but may not change it. All failed cases remain in the denominator. The matched no-service replay is the counterfactual for the limited 24-hour synthetic attribution; a service is called unnecessary only when that same-seed no-service run has no same-asset failure in the declared horizon. If either replay fails, attribution is undetermined.

Final release audit: all 24 declared final cases retained; 253 full-suite tests passed after final evaluation; 3530 protected upstream hashes unchanged. Policy/design/model parameters were not modified. Reporting ledgers and final validation tables are EVALUATION-ONLY and cannot enter PredictionService. Failed full-day continuations are not independently accepted physical plans.
