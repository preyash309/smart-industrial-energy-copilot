# Phase II-F pre-implementation physical audit

The frozen V1.1 scenario creates calendar-age wear for `MILL_01` and `PMP_01`. Each asset receives a seeded life draw of 13–16 days, accumulates wear to a threshold, then has a seeded corrective repair of 0.25–0.5 hours. Repair resets wear and starts another seeded life. This is a corrective-failure process, not a preventive-maintenance control. The existing `calendar.maintenance_dates` switches off a whole plant day; it does not service an individual asset or improve its condition. The replay-compatible layer admits heat starts, practice and rolling feed, but no individual service action, duration or condition reset.

The successor must therefore carry an explicit `SYNTHETIC-MAINTENANCE` intervention. A valid preventive service reserves the named asset for its configured duration; a pump service cannot overlap any furnace heat, and a mill service stops rolling. At service end, synthetic wear returns to a configured value and a new pre-generated life draw governs the next threshold. Corrective failures outside service remain possible. The original charge, practice, IF fault, weather, observation and pre-generated asset-life streams keep their seed identities. With no service control, the successor must reproduce the frozen Phase-II-E executor tables and scenario identity exactly. With service, a separate action-aware physical hash and the original exogenous hash must both be recorded.

No industrial service duration, restart time or cost is sourced in the frozen plant specification. Any chosen values are simulation assumptions, explicitly versioned. The frozen health models supply 24-hour probabilities from posted public sensors only; their classification thresholds are not maintenance decision thresholds. The future failure event and hidden wear draw are evaluation-only and cannot select a service window. A matched no-service failure can be called avoided only when the service physically resets wear before its otherwise-matched threshold. A false preventive service is one whose same-seed no-service counterfactual had no relevant failure in the declared evaluation window.

The frozen one-day E4 controller has no maintenance event loop or dynamic asset availability. A successor needs independent service timing, immutable executed-service records, a maintenance-compatible replay, and a checker that recomputes machine availability, production, inventory, demand and cooling without reading the MILP constraint expressions. Day 7 has no meaningful maintenance-outage evidence; later predeclared working days from full 30-day trajectories are required. Existing E4 outcomes and all Phase I–II-E files remain immutable.

Final release audit: all 24 declared final cases retained; 253 full-suite tests passed after final evaluation; 3530 protected upstream hashes unchanged. Policy/design/model parameters were not modified. Reporting ledgers and final validation tables are EVALUATION-ONLY and cannot enter PredictionService. Failed full-day continuations are not independently accepted physical plans.
